Self Characteristics · Body Structure

Body Type
Reference

There are five body structures. The key is to identify where your widest horizontal line falls — shoulder, waist, or hip — and whether your outline reads as straight or curved.

These reference images show the full range within each structure. Use them to confirm your own identification.

The Round Body

The waist line is wider than both the shoulder and hip lines. Fullness is carried through the center. This structure calls for garments that create horizontal emphasis at the shoulder and thigh, and a flowing A-line silhouette through the hip.

The Triangle

The broadest line is the hip — usually placed at or below the hip line. The upper body is narrower than the lower body. Garments that create volume and structure at the shoulder and bust balance this structure while keeping the hip area clean and fitted.

The Inverted
Triangle

Shoulders are wider than the waist and hip lines. The upper body carries the structural emphasis. Garments with deep V-necks, angular cuts, and hip-line detailing draw attention downward and create visual balance between the broader upper frame and the narrower lower body.

The Hourglass

Shoulder and hip width are equal, with a clearly narrower natural waist. Garments that outline the waist at its narrowest point — belts, cinched tops, fitted silhouettes — reinforce this structure's inherent balance. This is the rarest of the five structures.

The Rectangle

Shoulder and hip lines are close in width with a less defined waist. The silhouette reads as relatively straight and balanced. Garments with angular cuts, side tucks, and structured lines that create the appearance of waist definition work best for this structure.
